# DAWLYY can win by pairing Arabic-first coaching with automated payments

DAWLYY should launch as a Next.js 16 web app and offline-capable PWA on Supabase Postgres in Frankfurt. It should use Drizzle with row-level security, Paymob for payments, WhatsApp for authentication and reminders, and coach-approved AI drafting. No competitor combines the three things the evidence shows are open: a native Arabic/RTL client experience, fully automated Egyptian payments, and nutrition that fits local food. Hevy and Strong set the standard for set logging but offer almost nothing for coaches. Beprime and FitXpert prove Egyptian coaches will pay for software, yet both confirm payments by hand. The global platforms (Trainerize, Everfit, Kahunas) bill per client plus stacked add-ons, and none evidences Arabic support. Egypt adds hard launch constraints: the personal-data law's enforcement window opened around 1 November 2026, health data likely counts as sensitive, and hosting abroad needs a transfer permit. The stack below is sourced from official pages read on 7 November 2026. Four items need a one-day spike before the lock: Cairo-to-Frankfurt latency, Egyptian SMS cost, Arabic PDF on Vercel, and iOS PWA offline behaviour. Market-size and fee figures here are estimates. Coach pricing in EGP is unknown and needs 20-30 coach interviews.

## Hevy and Strong set the logging bar, but neither coaches

Both apps rate **4.9 on the US App Store** (Hevy from 102K ratings, Strong from 109K) ([Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-gym-tracker-workout-log/id1458862350), [Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577)). Hevy shows the last session's weight and reps per exercise and sets rest timers per exercise. It tags warm-up, drop and failure sets, and its 2026 releases added an RPE colour scale, pinned notes and a zero-rep warning (same Hevy listing). Strong's differentiators are Live Activity and Dynamic Island rest timers (6.5.0) and Apple Watch that works without the phone (same Strong listing). Hevy also ships a Wear OS app, and Strong has none ([SensAI Strong review](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/strong-app-review-2026)). Several of these comparison sources are written by competing apps, so treat the finer details as indicative.

Their weaknesses are structural. Free tiers cap routines at 4 (Hevy) and 3 (Strong), and Hevy limits free graph history to 3 months ([SensAI Hevy review](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/hevy-review-2026)). Strong gates plate and warm-up calculators behind Pro. Sync is a repeated weak spot: Strong's 2026 notes include sign-in, sync and large-history freezing fixes, and Hevy users report duplicate Apple Health and feed entries ([r/Hevy mirror](https://axebps-redlib.hf.space/r/Hevy/comments/1jjh1ab/duplicate_posts_in_activity_app)). Strong has no coach product. Hevy Coach costs about **$25 a month** for 1-10 clients with clients using the normal app, and its homepage mentions no check-ins, forms, nutrition or payments ([hevycoach.com](http://hevycoach.com/)). Hevy already lists Arabic among 16 languages, so Arabic in the logging screen is table stakes, not a differentiator ([Hevy EG App Store](https://apps.apple.com/eg/app/hevy-gym-tracker-workout-log/id1458862350)).

DAWLYY should copy the logging patterns outright: prefilled previous values, per-exercise rest timer with live plus-or-minus 15 seconds, set tags, optional RPE and RIR, pinned notes and per-exercise history. It should keep these free for coached clients and add what neither documents, which is coach feedback on individual sets. The only concrete engineering answer to the duplicate and lost-data complaints is client-generated IDs per set and workout, which is an inference from the evidence rather than a documented rival method.

## Beprime and FitXpert prove demand, and both still confirm payments by hand

Beprime is a platform company, not a coach brand. "Captain Maged" is one tenant: the captainmaged.com code resolves tenants from `*.beprime.site` hostnames and sends tenant headers ([captainmaged.com](https://www.captainmaged.com/)). Beprime charges coaches **1,000 EGP a month for up to 100 clients** and 2,000 EGP for the next tier, with enterprise custom ([beprime.ink](https://www.beprime.ink/)). Payment is a Vodafone Cash or InstaPay transfer, then a WhatsApp receipt, with activation within 24 working hours; refunds are WhatsApp-only and limited to 7 days with no clients added ([refund policy](https://www.beprime.ink/refund-policy)). The Captain Maged iOS app has **4.27 stars from 11 ratings**, an English-only listing and a "Data Not Collected" privacy label that is implausible for a login-and-chat app ([App Store EG](https://apps.apple.com/eg/app/captain-maged/id6756163707)). The identity of the coach and the operator is inferred, not confirmed. "B Prime" most likely means Beprime. The spec's "FitStation" is ambiguous: a per-coach Egyptian app called Fitstn exists ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/fitstn/6739849951)) alongside an unrelated US FitStation, so the spec author must confirm which was meant.

FitXpert is the funded incumbent. It was founded in 2023 and announced a seven-figure investment from Foras Investment in January 2026, with Gulf expansion planned ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2026/01/fitxpert-secures-seven-figure-backing-scale-fitness-nutrition-saas-platform)). Its feature set covers the Egyptian coach workflow: client profiles, check-in forms, a builder with supersets and circuits, 1000+ exercise videos, a 1000+ item food database, challenges, team permissions and automated renewals ([landing page](https://landing.fitxpert.team/)). G2 shows **4.6 from 47 reviews** with "occasional technical issues" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/fitxpert)). The shared client app has 4.4 stars from 61 Google Play reviews and 5K+ downloads ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.fitxpert.teams)). Scale claims disagree: 400+ coaches in a May 2025 press piece versus 1000+ on the landing page, so the paying base is probably in the hundreds ([IssueWire](https://www.issuewire.com/mostafa-mahmoud-the-young-entrepreneur-redefining-healthtech-and-fitness-in-the-mena-region-1830782216764459)). Its pricing is USD client bands: free for 3 clients, then roughly $19 to $80 a month for 10 to 250 clients, with a 14-day no-card trial ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/fitxpert)). One coach page sells packages via InstaPay, Visa and Fawry with manual bank-transfer confirmation within 24 working hours ([coach page](https://ziad-mm.codebase-product.com/transformations/1)). A shipped iOS regression that removed decimal entry for weight ([App Store EG](https://apps.apple.com/eg/app/fitxpert-app/id6673904408)) shows the cost of moving fast without regression tests.

The pattern across both is the opening. Neither automates payment reconciliation, neither evidences AI, and both ship thin trust signals. FitXpert's Gulf move means DAWLYY's KSA entry will meet it, so differentiation must be coach workflow and reliability, not Arabic alone. DAWLYY must still match their baseline: Arabic dashboard and app, a free tier or no-card trial, subscription reminders, check-ins, progress photos, a 1000+ exercise library and macro-calculating diet builder.

## Global platforms charge for add-ons and show no Arabic support

Per-client tiers with paid add-ons are the norm. Trainerize runs from a free single-client plan to $248 a month for studios, with Advanced Nutrition at $20-45, Stripe payments at $10 and a Custom Branded App at $169 one-time on Pro, and its own page contradicts itself on some prices ([Trainerize](https://www.trainerize.com/pricing/)). Everfit starts at $19 a month, charges add-ons per client for automation, and reserves its own-store white-label app for Enterprise (500+ clients) ([Everfit](https://everfit.io/pricing/)). Kahunas moved to per-seat pricing with white-label only from 200 seats ([Coachway](https://coachway.io/articles/kahunas-pricing/)); other sources still show older flat tiers. Coach complaints centre on per-client pricing and add-on stacking, plus post-acquisition Trainerize bugs ([aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/?p=5911)). Those sources are mostly competitor-authored or secondhand. Reddit, G2 and Facebook threads were not retrievable.

Nutrition is the weakest layer. Everfit logs manually or syncs MyFitnessPal and has no native food database described ([Promealplan](https://www.promealplan.com/en/blog/everfit-review-2026)). Trainerize's MyFitnessPal sync is reported as unreliable on and off ([aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/?p=5911)). Neither evidences household measures or regional dishes. On language, an RTL request on Trainerize's ideas portal has had one vote and no reply since September 2023 ([ABC ideas portal](https://ideas.abcfitness.com/forums/167887-coach-trainer-abc-trainerize/suggestions/47245526-right-to-left-language-support)). Everfit is English-only and Kahunas English-first. This is absence of evidence, not proof, and nobody tested each client app in an Arabic locale. Liaqa claims RTL readiness in its listing but shows no ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ly/app/liaqa/id6736395398)). "Hupe (formerly Harder)" could not be found, and the closest match, Harder Coaching, appears to be a coach brand on Kahunas, so the premise is unverified.

AI is now table stakes, not a moat. Trainerize says over 40% of workouts created are AI-generated (a vendor claim) and launched FitMetrics in September 2026 for check-in analysis and drafting ([ABC Fitness](https://abcfitness.com/?p=30257)). Everfit ships AI-drafted replies in the coach's tone ([Everfit](https://everfit.io/pricing/)). DAWLYY's defensible position is coach-controlled AI, which its spec already mandates, together with Arabic and local food. The remaining spec features (coach-defined assessments, explainable decision queue with risk flags) show up in no benchmark, but that is an absence finding from limited reading. Test it with coaches before betting on it.

## Egypt at launch requires Paymob, WhatsApp, a PDPC licence and a built food table

No credible Egypt-specific market figure exists. Statista's broader health-and-wellness coaching figure of US$104m for 2024 is an estimate behind a paywall ([Statista](https://statista.com/outlook/dmo/digital-health/digital-fitness-well-being/health-wellness-coaching/egypt)). Size the market bottom-up from coach counts times seat price instead.

**Payments.** Paymob documents tokenised subscriptions with weekly through annual cycles and retries for failed payments ([Paymob docs](https://developers.paymob.com/paymob-docs/payments-and-features/core-features/subscriptions)). It is profitable and raised $22m in 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/11/paymob-lands-another-22-million-and-is-profitable-in-egypt)). Fees near 2.75% come from comparison sites, not an official schedule ([PaymentProviders.io](https://paymentproviders.io/country/eg)). Fawry adds cash codes for the unbanked. Stripe does not onboard Egypt-registered businesses directly, and the usual workaround is a foreign entity ([Stripe guide](https://foundlie-web.pages.dev/blog/stripe-guide-for-egyptians), unofficial). Merchant-of-record services exist but the cited source has a stake ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/paymob-alternatives)). Kashier and Geidea were not priced, and Vodafone Cash and InstaPay merchant APIs were not found, so launch those as manual proof upload at first. Egypt charges 14% VAT with ETA e-receipts since January 2025, per a vendor blog that should be checked with ETA (same Dodo source).

**Messaging.** WhatsApp utility and authentication messages to Egypt cost roughly **$0.004 each**, with marketing at $0.06-0.08. Two vendor sources disagree slightly ([ChatMaxima](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/egypt/), [Chatalog](https://docs.chatalog.ai/platform-setup/whatsapp-connection-guide/whatsapp-pricing-update)). A vendor guide puts Twilio SMS to Egypt at about **$0.40**, which would make SMS OTP up to 100 times costlier ([Sent.dm](https://sent.dm/resources/egypt-sms-pricing)). Default to WhatsApp OTP or email magic link with SMS as fallback, apply for a business solution provider (BSP) and Meta verification early, and launch with utility and authentication templates only.

**Food data.** The only official table found is the National Nutrition Institute's 1996 edition (second edition 2006), apparently not open data ([FAO INFOODS](https://www.fao.org/infoods/infoods/tables-and-databases/egypt/en)). Open Food Facts is open but licensed under ODbL with share-alike, so counsel must check how it merges into a proprietary table ([wiki](https://wiki.openfoodfacts.org/ODBL_License)). Egyptian coverage and household measures are unmeasured everywhere. Budget manual curation of common dishes with measures such as spoon, cup and loaf, and contact the NNI about licensing. Arabic training terminology is also unsourced, and the glossary must come from 20-30 real coach programs.

**Legal and hosting.** Executive Regulations to Law 151/2020 were issued on 1 November 2025 with a one-year compliance countdown, so enforcement is plausibly live now. Whether the regulator extended the deadline is unconfirmed ([CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins)). Obligations include a controller licence, a cross-border transfer permit (cloud included), written Arabic-language consent for sensitive data, a registered data protection officer and 72-hour breach notice to the regulator (same CMS source). Weight, body photos and injuries are likely sensitive, which is an inference, not stated by the source. A Frankfurt database is a cross-border transfer, so Egyptian counsel must decide between a transfer permit and in-Egypt hosting before launch. Fines were not covered by the fetched source. Infrastructure is workable either way: CloudFront has had a Cairo edge since May 2024 ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/05/new-edge-location-egypt)), and no measured Cairo latency to any region was found.

## The stack to lock: Next.js, Supabase Frankfurt, Drizzle and an outbox

The stack notes validate the leading candidate with four adjustments. **Next.js 16.4** (6 October 2026) is the stable line, with Cache Components recommended for new apps and default in Next.js 17 ([Next.js 16.4](https://nextjs.org/blog/next-16-4)). Security releases arrive about monthly, with out-of-band fixes on 22 September and 14 October 2026 ([Next.js blog](https://nextjs.org/blog)), so pin the version and reserve a monthly upgrade slot. Vercel Hobby is non-commercial only, so budget **Vercel Pro at $20 a month** from the first paying customer ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/pricing)). **Supabase Pro at $25 a month** covers 100,000 MAU, 8 GB database and 100 GB storage ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)). No Middle East or Africa region exists, so Frankfurt is the likely nearest by geography, unmeasured ([regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)). Multi-tenancy rides on row-level security: index every policy column, wrap auth calls in `select`, and use security-definer helpers ([RLS guide](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security)). **Drizzle** declares policies in TypeScript beside the schema ([Drizzle](https://orm.drizzle.team/docs/rls)). Test isolation with SQL tests regardless, and remember that a service-role connection bypasses RLS. Supabase had several 2026 incidents, including a 3h42m outage on 12 February ([postmortem](https://supabase.com/blog/supabase-incident-on-february-12-2026)), so keep daily backups outside Supabase.

Offline logging is the riskiest piece. iOS Web Push needs a Home Screen install on iOS 16.4 or later ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)). Secondary sources report no iOS Background Sync, and they disagree on storage eviction for installed apps ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)). Design the outbox to flush in the foreground, with a UUID idempotency key on every queued set and an upsert on the server. Add a visible unsynced indicator and a manual sync button. Serwist has a Turbopack package, but its docs do not mention Next.js 16, so spike it ([Serwist](https://serwist.pages.dev/docs/next/turbo)). Large video uploads should use foreground TUS resumable uploads with 6 MB chunks ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/uploads/resumable-uploads)).

For the front end, Tailwind 4.3 has `rtl:` variants and shadcn/ui has an RTL migration, though Calendar, Pagination and Sidebar need manual fixes ([Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/hover-focus-and-other-states), [shadcn/ui](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/rtl)). Use logical utilities from day one. Arabic PDF export through headless Chromium shapes Arabic correctly only if Arabic fonts are bundled, because `@sparticuz/chromium` ships only Open Sans ([README](https://github.com/Sparticuz/chromium)). Smoke-test a deployed Arabic PDF with numerals, mixed English names and tables, and keep a Gotenberg-style container as fallback. For automations, pg_cron with Supabase Queues costs nothing extra, and Trigger.dev Hobby at $10 a month is the cheapest paid step with real headroom ([Trigger.dev](https://trigger.dev/pricing)). Inngest's free tier pauses silently at 50k executions ([Inngest](https://www.inngest.com/pricing)). Resend Pro at $20 a month covers email ([Resend](https://resend.com/pricing)). For coach-approved drafts, Haiku 5.5 at $0.10 input and $0.50 output per million tokens, escalating to Sonnet 5.5 at $2 and $10, keeps cost low ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Arabic quality of either model was not tested.

Fixed monthly cost at launch is about **$45 to $75** before SMS and AI usage: Vercel $20, Supabase $25, optional Resend $20 and Trigger.dev $10. That is arithmetic on cited prices only. The alternatives (Flutter and Firebase, Laravel, FastAPI and React, Expo universal) were not researched, and their rejection is reasoned judgement. Flutter and Firebase lacks SQL tenant isolation. FastAPI would make the founder own auth, storage and queues. Laravel is the credible fallback if PHP is the founder's strength. Expo is better kept as the later native client over the shared API.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the competition. The threat is not feature count, since FitXpert already matches most of the table stakes and the global tools out-feature everyone. The threat is operational trust: manual payment confirmation, regressions, and add-on billing. DAWLYY wins by making the boring parts automatic. A coach should be able to sell a package, receive money, activate the client and see renewals without WhatsApp receipts. That is the one gap confirmed independently at Beprime and at a FitXpert coach page.

The biggest risk to the plan is not technical. Egypt's data-protection regime may force a hosting decision that changes both the Supabase region and the launch date, and nobody has yet confirmed the enforcement timing. Resolve that with Egyptian counsel first, then run the one-day spike on latency, SMS cost, Arabic PDF and iOS offline behaviour, and interview 20-30 coaches on pricing, delivery habits, food measures and terminology. Until those are done, treat the Arabic-nutrition and decision-queue advantages as hypotheses, not facts.
